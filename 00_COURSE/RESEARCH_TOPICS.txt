HOW A TOPIC IS OPENED (BP-13: QUESTIONS FIRST)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

A research topic begins at a source -- usually a lecture timestamp --
and is opened by an opening note, Pnn-YYYY-MM-DD-RTnn, written in
this order. Conclusions come last.

  I.   Source and claim     What was said or shown, quoted, with the
                            timestamped link and the frame if any.
  II.  Assumptions          What the source states it assumes, and
                            what it must assume without saying so.
  III. Opening questions    The standard nine below, made specific.
  IV.  Preliminary analysis Marked as preliminary, and by whom.
  V.   Side projects        Anything that would take the work off
                            the main line (DEC-00, R-07).
  VI.  Critical review      As in every document.

The standard opening questions:

  Q1  Definition    What exactly is claimed? By what criterion?
  Q2  Mechanism     How does it work, at the level of an example?
  Q3  Conditions    What must be supplied for it to hold?
  Q4  Scope         What does it not cover?
  Q5  Dispute       What question was raised against it, and what is
                    the sharpest answer?
  Q6  Lens          What is the nearest engineering practice, and
                    where does the comparison fail?
  Q7  Cost          What is paid, and when is it worth paying?
  Q8  Implication   What follows for future technology?
  Q9  Refutation    What observation would show it false or narrower?

A topic that needs long exploration is not pursued in the session.
It is proposed as a side project (DEC-00, R-06).

Status values: OPEN (questions posed); ACTIVE (under discussion);
POSITION (the author's position is recorded -- cite the record);
PARKED (moved to a side project).
