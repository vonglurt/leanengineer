II. PURPOSE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The notebook is a hybrid of two things.

1) Class lecture notes. The first project, P01, follows the public
   lecture series "Lean for Scientists and Engineers, Summer 2024" by
   Tyler Josephson [1], and learns the Lean 4 theorem prover from it.

2) Philosophical analysis. Most of the working time goes to what the
   material means: the future implications of these technologies, and
   new ways of thinking, worked out by applying them to the symbolic
   notation of machine-checked proof.

The method is the engineer's: divide a behavior into regions, classify
each region, and tie each class to something that can be measured --
as piecewise analysis turns a nonlinear characteristic into regions
with empirical parameters.

III. AUDIENCE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every document is written for two readers at once.

A. The Expert Professor

An expert who needs reminding, not teaching. The reader is trusted to
follow full technical precision and to see the general application
without being led to it. Reminders are given by reference to source
material, not by tutorial exposition. The reader is assumed fluent
in:

  - circuit theory; mesh and nodal analysis; two-port networks and
    their H (hybrid) and S (scattering) parameters;
  - advanced matrix mathematics, including complex matrices;
  - complex analysis;
  - partial differential equations;
  - Fourier and Laplace transforms and their inverses; s-domain
    analysis; impulse response and the Dirac delta; steady-state
    analysis;
  - control systems theory, including PID control, and the solution
    of systems of equations by control-theoretic methods;
  - probability and number systems;
  - mechanics and dynamics.

Analogies are therefore drawn from these fields without preamble, and
each states where it breaks down (BP-10).

B. The Superintelligent Reader

A reader, human or machine, able to check everything. For this reader
every claim is assumed to be referenceable and researchable, so every
claim carries its source, and every source carries the assumptions on
which it rests (rule R-02). Nothing is asserted on authority alone.

IV. STANDING RULES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

R-01  Questions first. A new research topic opens with a written set
      of questions before any conclusions (BP-13). The topic is
      entered in 00_COURSE/RESEARCH_TOPICS.txt. The first use is
      RT-01 [P01-2026-11-25-RT01].

R-02  Sources and their assumptions. Every claim cites a source, and
      every source entry records what that source assumes (BP-14).
      A claim with no source is marked as the author's own reasoning.

R-03  Lecture timestamps. A moment in a lecture is cited as
      [Lnn @ h:mm:ss] with its share link,
      https://youtu.be/<video id>?t=<seconds>. If the author gives a
      timestamp without the link, the assistant asks for the link
      (BP-15).

R-04  Screenshots. The assistant takes them. A lecture frame is
      captured from the local video file at the cited second
      ("make shot"); a screenshot of the author's own screen is taken
      or filed on request. Each becomes a figure file: the image, an
      ASCII transcription of what it shows, and an analysis (BP-15).

R-05  Declaration and chat history. This declaration and the chat
      history are kept. Every published document cites DEC-00 and the
      chat log or logs in which it was worked out (BP-17). Chat logs
      are kept raw and edited, as set out in SP-00, Section III-F.

R-06  Working mode. The assistant researches and thinks in the
      session, synchronously, with the author reading along and
      taking notes. It does not spawn background agents. A long
      exploration is not started on the assistant's own initiative:
      it is proposed to the author as a side research project
      (R-07), for which another agent may then be granted permission.
      Questions are asked at the start, together, each with a
      recommendation; the author will generally take the logical
      best choice for an engineer.

R-07  Side projects. Ideas for later work go to
      00_COURSE/SIDE_PROJECTS.txt as they arise, with their origin,
      so that the main line of study is not interrupted.

R-08  Form. ASCII text, hard line returns, IEEE report structure,
      critical review last, tables and figures as cited files, notes
      filed by project and by date -- as defined in SP-00.

R-09  Repository and deployment. The notebook is a git repository
      published at the address above. "make deploy" is defined as:
      (1) run the format checks and stop on any failure; (2) stage
      everything not excluded by .gitignore; (3) commit, if anything
      is staged; (4) push to origin main. It never forces a push,
      rewrites history, or publishes local-only material.

R-10  License and third-party material. The MIT license covers the
      author's own notes, analysis, and code. Lecture videos,
      transcripts, and slides belong to their authors. Full
      transcripts and raw video frames are kept in 99_LOCAL/, which
      is not published; the notes quote short passages with
      timestamped links and publish only descriptions of frames
      (BP-16). This rule was recommended by the assistant and awaits
      the author's confirmation (A-01).

VI. CRITICAL REVIEW AND BEST PRACTICES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. What Works

The declaration separates what is fixed (purpose, audience, rules)
from what changes (plans, syllabus, notes), so later documents can
cite it without restating it.

B. Weaknesses and Risks

1) Two audiences can pull apart: the expert wants brevity, the
   checking reader wants every assumption stated. Resolution adopted:
   brevity in the prose, completeness in the references.
2) Rule R-10 limits what is published. Should the author prefer to
   publish frames or transcripts, the lecturer's permission or a
   stated license is needed first; the transcripts on hand record
   "License: not stated".
3) Two readings in Section V are the assistant's (A-02).

C. Best Practices Incorporated

BP-13 (questions first), BP-14 (expert reader; source assumptions),
BP-15 (timestamped, transcribed evidence), BP-16 (third-party
material stays local; proposed), BP-17 (every published document
cites DEC-00 and its chat logs). Sources: this declaration; [2].
