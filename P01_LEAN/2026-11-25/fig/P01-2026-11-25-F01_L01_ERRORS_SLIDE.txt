ANALYSIS

1) The table has two intervention columns. "Intervention" is
   conventional practice; "Lean" is the lecturer's proposal. The
   conventional column moves later and gets costlier down the rows:
   editor, then a run, then a human. The Lean column is constant.
   The slide's whole argument is that one column is uniform.

2) The categories are distinguished by the stage at which an error
   is detected, not by its cause. The slide therefore describes a
   re-staging of detection, not a removal of causes.

3) The Semantic row is the strong claim. "Missing a minus sign" is
   an error only against an intended result. The slide does not show
   where that intent is written down; in Lean it must be a stated
   theorem. The cell reads "Editor" on the unstated condition that
   a specification exists.

4) The slide omits a fourth category that the lecturer adds in
   speech in the same chapter: floating-point and round-off error,
   which he places outside the scope of the course.
